## 2. Problem Definition

Medium-range Numerical Weather Prediction (NWP) forecasts can experience significant errors during rapidly evolving weather systems such as monsoon depressions, heavy rainfall events, western disturbances, cyclones, heat waves, and active/break monsoon phases.

These forecast failures, commonly referred to as **forecast busts**, can affect operational planning and weather-sensitive decision-making.

The proposed system will develop an **AI/ML-based forecast reliability layer** that analyses current NWP forecast patterns against historical forecast-error behaviour to identify:

* Regions where forecasts are likely to be unreliable.
* Forecast lead times with increased uncertainty.
* Probability of a significant forecast error.
* Meteorological and model-related factors contributing to low confidence.

The system will not replace the existing NWP forecast. Instead, it will provide an additional **confidence and forecast-bust assessment layer**.

---

# 3. Product Vision

The system will provide an interactive web-based operational dashboard that converts complex NWP and historical forecast-error information into an easy-to-understand:

**Forecast → Error Risk → Confidence → Explanation**

workflow.

The platform should allow users to select a forecast lead time from **Day 1 to Day 10**, inspect region-wise confidence, identify areas with high forecast-bust probability, and understand the primary factors responsible for reduced confidence.

---

# 4. Objectives

The primary objectives are:

1. Analyse historical NWP forecast errors.
2. Identify patterns associated with forecast busts.
3. Predict the probability of a significant forecast error.
4. Generate region-wise forecast confidence.
5. Provide confidence information for Day 1–Day 10.
6. Identify error-prone geographical areas.
7. Provide explainable reasons for low confidence.
8. Visualise forecast reliability through an interactive map.
9. Provide APIs for accessing forecast-confidence information.
10. Develop a prototype suitable for operational decision-support use.

# 6. Functional Requirements

## FR-01 — Weather/NWP Data Ingestion

The system shall ingest available forecast and observation datasets.

The system should support:

* NWP forecast data
* Historical forecast data
* Observed weather data
* Atmospheric variables
* Geographic/grid information
* Ensemble forecast information where available

The system should maintain the relationship between:

**Forecast → Observation → Forecast Error**

---

## FR-02 — Data Preprocessing

The system shall perform:

* Missing-value handling
* Data validation
* Time alignment
* Spatial/grid alignment
* Unit normalization
* Duplicate detection
* Outlier handling where appropriate

The processed data shall be converted into a standardized format suitable for machine-learning models.

---

## FR-03 — Forecast Error Calculation

The system shall compare forecast values with corresponding observations.

Example:

**Forecast rainfall:** 100 mm
**Observed rainfall:** 40 mm

**Absolute error:**

`|100 − 40| = 60 mm`

The system shall calculate appropriate error metrics depending on the forecast variable, including:

* Absolute Error
* MAE
* RMSE
* Relative/percentage error where appropriate
* Other domain-relevant error metrics

---

## FR-04 — Forecast Bust Identification

The system shall identify significant forecast errors using predefined and data-driven thresholds.

A forecast bust shall be represented as a classification target such as:

* `0 = No significant bust`
* `1 = Significant forecast bust`

Thresholds should be configurable and validated against historical forecast behaviour.

---

## FR-05 — Feature Engineering

The system shall generate predictive features from historical and current forecast information.

Potential features include:

* Forecast value
* Historical forecast error
* Rolling MAE/RMSE
* Forecast lead time
* Forecast run-to-run variation
* Atmospheric pressure
* Temperature
* Humidity
* Wind speed/direction
* Rainfall
* Geopotential height
* CAPE
* Vertical wind shear
* Soil moisture
* Ensemble spread
* Regional information
* Historical regional forecast reliability

---

## FR-06 — AI/ML Forecast Bust Prediction

The system shall use machine-learning models to estimate the probability of a forecast bust.

The initial prototype may evaluate models such as:

* Logistic Regression
* Random Forest
* XGBoost

The model shall output:

**Bust Probability**

Example:

`Bust Probability = 0.73`

which represents a model-estimated 73% probability of a significant forecast error under the defined target and validation methodology.

---

## FR-07 — Forecast Error Prediction

Where sufficient training data is available, the system should additionally predict the expected magnitude of forecast error.

Example:

**Expected rainfall forecast error: 42 mm**

This provides a continuous error estimate in addition to the bust classification.

---

## FR-08 — Forecast Confidence Generation

The system shall convert forecast-bust probability into a confidence indicator.

For the prototype:

**Confidence = 1 − Bust Probability**

Example:

`Bust Probability = 35%`

`Confidence = 65%`

The confidence value shall be clearly identified as the system's **AI-derived forecast reliability indicator**, rather than an official NWP forecast.

The system shall provide forecast confidence at a geographical/grid level.

---

## FR-11 — Forecast Confidence Map

The system shall provide an interactive map of India showing spatial forecast reliability.

The map shall visually distinguish confidence levels.

Example:

* High confidence
* Moderate confidence
* Low confidence
* Very low confidence

Users shall be able to click a region/grid cell to view detailed information.

---

## FR-12 — Error-Prone Area Detection

The system shall identify geographical areas where the current forecast has a comparatively high probability of error.

The dashboard should provide a list such as:

| Region   | Lead Time | Bust Probability | Risk     |
| -------- | --------: | ---------------: | -------- |
| Region A |     Day 5 |              72% | High     |
| Region B |     Day 5 |              61% | High     |
| Region C |     Day 5 |              43% | Moderate |

The ordering and thresholds shall be based on the system's defined metrics rather than subjective judgement.

---

## FR-13 — Explainable AI

The system shall provide explanations for low-confidence predictions.

Potential contributing factors include:

* High historical forecast error
* High ensemble spread
* Large forecast run-to-run change
* Long forecast lead time
* Rapid atmospheric changes
* Unusual weather patterns
* Region-specific historical instability

Where applicable, explainability techniques such as **SHAP** may be used to identify influential model features.

Example:

> **Low Confidence**
>
> Bust Probability: 76%
>
> Main contributing factors:
>
> * High ensemble spread
> * Significant forecast change from previous run
> * Elevated historical rainfall error
> * Rapid pressure variation

---


# 15. Success Criteria

The prototype will be considered successful when it can:

* Process historical forecast and observation data.
* Calculate forecast errors.
* Identify significant forecast busts using the defined methodology.
* Generate a validated bust-probability prediction.
* Generate region-wise confidence information.
* Provide Day 1–Day 10 confidence analysis.
* Display forecast reliability on an interactive map.
* Identify areas with elevated forecast-bust probability.
* Explain major factors associated with low-confidence predictions.
* Expose predictions through an API.
* Present the results through a usable operational dashboard.

---

# 16. Core Product Output

The final system should answer four questions:

### 1. WHERE?

**Which regions have potentially unreliable forecasts?**

### 2. WHEN?

**At which forecast lead times is reliability reduced?**

### 3. HOW MUCH?

**What is the estimated probability of a significant forecast error?**

### 4. WHY?

**What factors are contributing to the reduced confidence?**

The final product therefore acts as an **AI-powered forecast reliability and forecast-bust detection layer over medium-range NWP forecasts**, rather than replacing the underlying weather forecasting system.
